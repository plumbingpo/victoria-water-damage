Preventing Water Heater Floods in Victoria, TX

Home / Blog / Preventing Water Heater Floods in Victoria, TX

In Victoria, TX, a failed water heater is behind a large share of the water damage we see in homes. A standard 40 to 50 gallon water heater that fails catastrophically can release its entire contents onto your property in minutes, causing thousands of dollars in damage to floors, walls, and stored belongings. Understanding the warning signs of impending failure and implementing preventive measures can save you from this costly emergency.

Signs Your Water Heater Is About to Fail

Watch for these indicators that your water heater may be nearing failure: age over 8 to 12 years (the typical lifespan for tank-style heaters), rust-colored water from hot taps, rumbling or popping sounds during heating cycles, visible corrosion or rust on the tank exterior, puddles or moisture around the base of the unit, reduced hot water capacity, and a relief valve that drips or discharges frequently.

Routine Care for Your Water Heater

A maintained heater lives longer and is less likely to fail, so once a year, flush the tank and get the built-up sediment out. The anode rod needs a look every 2 to 3 years and should be swapped out once it is significantly corroded. The pressure and temperature relief valve should be tested every year. Worn or corroded supply connections are worth checking for as well. Do not store things against the heater, since clear access makes inspection possible. A leak detection sensor placed by the base is worth considering.

Is It Time for a New Water Heater?

The most effective way to prevent a catastrophic water heater failure is proactive replacement before the unit fails. Frequent repairs, visible corrosion, or an age past 10 years are all reasons to replace the unit today and head off a flood later. The cost of a planned replacement is far less than the cost of emergency water damage restoration. With a tankless model, there is no tank to fail, so think about that upgrade.

Steps to Take After a Water Heater Flood

If your water heater fails, shut off the water supply to the unit immediately (the cold water inlet valve on top of the heater), then shut off the main water supply as backup.
