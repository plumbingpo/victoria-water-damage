Kitchen Water Damage: What Victoria Homeowners Should Know

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. For homeowners in Victoria, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Signs to Look For

  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

The Dishwasher Cavity

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. The appliance hides all of it until the floor changes.

The Ice Maker Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Under the Base Cabinets

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

Flooring Over Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.
