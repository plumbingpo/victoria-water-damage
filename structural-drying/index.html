Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at Victoria Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

How Our Structural Drying Works

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Equipment Deployment

Guided by the moisture map, we place high-velocity air movers and commercial LGR dehumidifiers where they will do the most good. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once

When to Call for Structural Drying in Victoria

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Materials that feel damp or cool to the touch even after visible water is gone
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
