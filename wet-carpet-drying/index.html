Wet Carpet Drying in Victoria

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Victoria homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Warning Signs

  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Subfloor Damage

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Furniture Marks

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Water Quality Drops

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

How We Fix It

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.
